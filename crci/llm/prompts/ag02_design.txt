## EXTRACTION INSTRUCTIONS

Classify the following study design characteristics:

1. **study_design**: The primary study design. Must be one of:
   - "RCT" — Randomized controlled trial
   - "longitudinal" — Longitudinal/prospective cohort study
   - "cross_sectional" — Cross-sectional study
   - "meta" — Meta-analysis or systematic review with pooled analysis
   - "intensive" — Intensive longitudinal design (EMA, daily diary)
   - "mechanistic" — Mechanistic/basic science study
   - "other" — Does not fit above categories

2. **randomization**: How participants were randomized. Examples:
   - "simple" — Simple randomization
   - "stratified" — Stratified randomization
   - "block" — Block randomization
   - "cluster" — Cluster randomization
   - "none" — No randomization (observational)
   - null — Cannot determine from text

3. **blinding**: Level of blinding. Examples:
   - "double-blind" — Neither participants nor assessors know allocation
   - "single-blind" — Participants OR assessors are blinded
   - "open-label" — No blinding
   - "assessor-blind" — Only outcome assessors are blinded
   - null — Cannot determine from text

4. **control_type**: Type of control/comparator group. Examples:
   - "placebo" — Placebo-controlled
   - "active" — Active comparator
   - "waitlist" — Waitlist control
   - "usual_care" — Usual care / standard of care
   - "no_treatment" — No treatment control
   - "attention" — Attention control
   - null — Cannot determine or not applicable

5. **arms**: List of study arms/groups. Extract exact names.
   Example: ["aerobic exercise", "resistance exercise", "usual care"]

6. **confidence**: Your confidence in the design classification (0.0 to 1.0).
   - 1.0: Design explicitly stated (e.g., "This was a double-blind RCT")
   - 0.7-0.9: Design strongly implied by methods description
   - 0.5-0.7: Design partially described, some inference needed
   - <0.5: Significant ambiguity

## RULES
- Classify based on what the paper EXPLICITLY describes
- If the Methods section describes randomization, it is likely an RCT
- Do NOT confuse meta-analysis (pooling multiple studies) with primary studies
- If blinding is not mentioned, set to null (do NOT assume open-label)
- Include ALL study arms, not just the primary comparison
- The CR-d hints above are preliminary — override them with your analysis
